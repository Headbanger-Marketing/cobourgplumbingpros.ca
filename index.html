Plan the request

Recording past water events before a new problem is assessed

A past leak, overflow or backup can leave a stain, a patched ceiling or a watermark that looks like a fresh fault. If your Cobourg home has a history like that, list each event with its date, its location and what was repaired. Add dated photos of old marks, so a provider can tell what may be old from what may be current, and decide what needs assessing.

  • Dated event list

    Write each past leak, backup or overflow with its approximate date, the room where it happened and who noticed it.

  • Repairs and patches

    Note what was repaired after each event, who did the work if you know, and any patched or repainted areas.

  • Photos of marks

    Take dated photos of old stains, tide lines and patches, with a fixed object in frame to show where each one sits.

Before you send

What to Photograph Before Requesting a Quote

A few clear photos help a provider understand the job before the first call. Have them ready for the follow-up.

  • Water heater: the label with the model and capacity, the vent connection at the top of the tank, and any rental sticker or agreement.
  • Blocked drain: which fixtures back up, whether it only happens on the lowest floor, and the floor drain or cleanout cap if you can find it.
  • Leak: the wet area or stain, the water meter with all fixtures turned off, and any recent work near the leak.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the pipe beside the water meter. Copper looks orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey and threaded, and lead is dull grey and turns shiny silver where you scratch it with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Cobourg Request

Should I mention a repaired leak in my Cobourg request?

Yes, if the repair left a mark or changed a fixture or pipe. Give the date, the room and what was done, even if you are unsure of details. Say what you do not know. The provider decides what the history means, and a short list that marks gaps is more useful than a guess.

What if I do not know my Cobourg home's water history?

Say so plainly and list only what you have seen yourself. Note visible stains, patches and repainted areas, and check any previous owner's notes or a home inspector's report for dated details. Unknown history is not a barrier to sending a request; it simply tells the provider where to look carefully.

Will old stains be treated as a new problem in my Cobourg request?

Sending dated photos helps show what is old, but the provider decides whether a mark is current. A stain you flag as past may still be checked, and one you did not mention may be questioned. Naming the date and repair keeps the conversation specific rather than assumed, before any scope is agreed.

Once I send my dated list of past water events, has a provider been found for my Cobourg address?

No. Your request, dated list or not, does not settle coverage, availability or price. Our enquiry team reviews it first, and a provider must then confirm the address, the work, written scope, pricing and scheduling before anything is agreed. Until then nothing has been booked and your past-event list is simply information you have shared.

How should I split old stains and a current drip into separate items?

Describe the current symptom first, then add a separate dated list for earlier events. If an old stain sits beside a new drip, photograph both and say which fixtures were running. Unrelated items, such as a softener question and a toilet repair, are clearer as separate requests, so each can be reviewed on its own.

Who decides whether a past water mark needs repair?

The provider who responds decides, after seeing the area and your records, and any repair needs a written scope you agree to. Your dated list and photos are evidence to support that decision, not a diagnosis. If a stain looks wet or is spreading, stop using the fixture nearest it where you can and say so in the request.
